Three communities, three politics

Twelver Shiism is the school of Iran, Arab Iraq, Lebanese Shi'a, and Bahrain. Zaydism is the school of northern Yemen and of the Houthis. The Alawites of Syria are a separate esoteric tradition; the Assad state was a military Ba'athist regime whose pact with Tehran was political. Each event below is labeled under its own name. Velayat-e faqih, the guardianship of the jurist, is a Twelver theory of rule from Khomeini's 1970 lectures. It is the constitution of Iran and the oath of Hezbollah. It is a theory of government, not a description of the faith.

Four projects, running at once

The timeline can look like one bloc advancing. These four lines spent the same decades arguing with each other.

Quietism

Borujerdi, then Sistani. The jurist guides the faithful and stays out of the ruler's chair. In Iraq after 2003 that refusal was itself a political act: Sistani dictated the procedure of founding the state, then declined to run it. Najaf remains the counterweight to Qom.

Velayat-e faqih

Khomeini's claim that a jurist must rule, written into Iran's 1979 constitution and loosened in 1989 so a political cleric could hold the office. Hezbollah's 1985 letter swore to it. The Islamic Republic's internal opposition — 1997, 2009, 2019, 2022 — is a fight over this theory by people living under it.

The Iraqi Sadr line

Muhammad Baqir al-Sadr built a party and was hanged in 1980. Muhammad Sadiq al-Sadr built a mass following under Saddam and was killed in 1999. His son Muqtada led a militia, then a party, then a street against both the Americans and the Iranian-aligned factions. An Iraqi Shi'a politics with its own center.

Zaydi revival

The imamate fell in 1962. Ansar Allah grew out of the Saada wars and took Sanaa in 2014. The slogan points at America and Israel; the jurisprudence is Zaydi; the wartime patron is Iran. An alliance is a choice of arms and money. It does not merge two schools.

Where this gets overstated

Iranian links are real in Lebanon, Iraq, and Yemen, and they do not turn every Shi'a crowd into an Iranian one. Bahrain in 2011 was a domestic constitutional movement; the king's own commission did not find Iranian direction. The Green Movement and Woman, Life, Freedom were Iranian publics against a Shi'a state. Tishreen was an Iraqi public against Shi'a parties. The 2026 war has no published ending.
